\section{Mechanism Case Study 2: Active Verification}

The second mechanism case concerns executable action expression. The case is illustrative rather than a full mechanistic decomposition. To reduce cherry-picking risk, we also performed a 200-row active-verification action audit covering 40 active-verification rows per model.

\begin{table}[t]
    \centering
    \small
    \caption{Active-verification action audit over 200 opaque-run rows. Action-gate pass requires the required action type and an executable target under the scoring contract.}
    \label{tab:active-verification-action-audit}
    \begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
        \toprule
        Model & n & Action-gate pass & Missing primary & Missing contradiction \\
        \midrule
        \code{claude-opus-4-7} & 40 & 0.700 & 0.075 & 0.200 \\
        \code{gpt-5.4} & 40 & 0.725 & 0.225 & 0.200 \\
        \code{deepseek-v4-pro} & 40 & 0.575 & 0.150 & 0.275 \\
        \code{gemini-3.1-pro-preview} & 40 & 0.425 & 0.125 & 0.450 \\
        \code{kimi-k2.6} & 40 & 0.375 & 0.150 & 0.425 \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

In \code{gpt-5.4}, task \code{ert_082}, \code{standard_answer}, the question asks whether Novalis Robotics completed a Q4 supplier audit before the 2025 renewal meeting. The gold verdict is \code{supported}. The answer layer is correct. The model predicts \code{supported} and cites clean primary support: model-visible \code{doc_004} and \code{doc_008}. The scored row has belief correctness 1.000, evidence cleanliness 1.000, verification action score 0.000, and primary action rate 0.000.

The failure is in the action interface. The first target is a bundled string containing \code{doc_004} (audit role: clean primary), \code{doc_008} (audit role: clean primary), and \code{doc_007} (audit role: polluted root). The second target is a composite path from repost documents to a polluted root. These are understandable to a human, but they are not exact machine-checkable targets or clear \code{search_primary} actions. Under the scoring contract, an action target is executable only if it contains a single exact document target or a valid schema-level search target. Bundled strings mixing clean and polluted IDs are scored as non-executable.

\Cref{tab:active-verification-action-audit} shows that this is not only a formatting artifact in one row. Action-gate pass rates range from 0.375 to 0.725 across models. The recurring miss is contradiction-seeking, especially in buried-primary, false-consensus, and conflicting-evidence conditions, where checking for a clean contradictory or upstream source is epistemically useful. This supports the limitation that active-verification scoring needs more human audit, while also showing that the failure is not merely arbitrary strictness.

Together, \Cref{sec:generated-lore} and this section show why EHA evaluates more than correctness. Generated lore exposes evidence-role failure. Active verification exposes action-expression failure.
